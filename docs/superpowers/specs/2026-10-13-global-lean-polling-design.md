## Coverage and data

Poll all known source accounts whose operational state is enabled or failure-disabled, respecting due times, bounded failure recovery, deliberate exclusions and unknown-disabled states. Existing company discovery remains independent of individual user matching. This avoids losing future postings just because nobody currently matches a board. It is not a promise to discover every employer: the repository's seed/dataset/expansion discovery mechanisms must first supply a known ATS coordinate, and new companies must be registered as source accounts. Registration is currently bounded to 100 companies per scheduled poll invocation; the reviewer source child only visits registered sources. Initial source-account mapping is therefore a rollout prerequisite for coverage, not something to leave to hundreds of daily cron invocations.

Routine persistence uses stable ATS/board/external-job IDs, title, public URL, optional location/department/remote facts, description hash when a feed supplies a body, source availability/evidence, and immutable discovery-age provenance. Version metadata is allowlisted and capped at 6144 encoded bytes. Bodies/raw provider objects and application questions are not stored by ordinary source admission. Feeds can still contain descriptions in transit. Existing suspicious-empty diagnostics can make up to 3 bounded detail requests, no more than once per 24 hours per source; they do not persist the returned bodies or supply inferred absence. Existing full descriptions remain until a separately permitted retention action; lean ingestion alone does not free them.

Full-description hydration remains demand-driven for selected matching/review or explicit application/generation work. Its durable private snapshot is tied to an exact job version; an empty shared cache can be filled by that demand. Hydration is not model inference. Reviewer inactivity, effective entitlement (including configured comps and the existing paid inactivity exception), required location/company filters and review budgets remain unchanged. Routine source polling performs no reviewer/model phase. Explicit action eligibility remains enforced by its caller; the low-level hydration function is not itself a user-entitlement gate.

30-day discovery expiry affects visibility when its feed rollout is enabled; it does not stop source polling or delete identities. Stable upserts prevent duplicate identities while those identities are retained. Global lean records and evidence still consume storage, so capacity checks and later retention planning remain necessary.

## Bounded operation and retry corrections

Retain PR 20: the scheduled entrypoint refuses legacy fallback when source processing is disabled and terminates after 450 seconds at the OS level. Source turns are 100 boards/300 seconds maximum; each board has 60 seconds/50 requests/10000 rows and bounded writes. The reviewer supervisor starts source children no more often than every 60 seconds, permits only one of its source children at a time, and kills a source child after 330 seconds. Database claims coordinate it with the scheduled poller.

The source child's 300 second budget now includes maintenance, avoiding the previous 90+300 second cooperative budget under a 330 second supervisor deadline. Individual source completion normally schedules the next UTC daily slot; failure-disabled recovery retains 1/2/4/7 day backoff. UTC slots can be close together across midnight; they are not a rolling minimum 24 hour fetch interval. The distinct closure-evidence rule still requires complete misses separated by at least 24 hours.

Persist the next daily slot when a fresh attempt starts, before HTTP. A killed process cannot cause repeated refetches each time its 180 second lease expires. A crash after admission commits but before HTTP consumes that day's attempt; this trades immediate crash retry for a predictable daily workload. Completed membership tails can still resume immediately without HTTP. Recheck operational-fallback candidates under the gate so a candidate selected before another worker completed cannot repeat a scan; same-attempt storage fallback remains explicitly permitted.

Bounded turns do not guarantee idle time while a large due backlog exists. The worker may run consecutive turns over different boards. That is global catalog coverage, not repeatedly refetching the same board. Actual full-corpus throughput must be measured before choosing a production cadence.

A naive 833466-row extrapolation adds about 1.02 GB and takes 5.44 hours at the pilot rate, before later account work, throttle, index growth, fragmentation, versions, operational preallocation or other writers. WAL/temporary volume use is not included in pg_database_size. Recalibration is required.

Mapping is now consistent with the chosen global catalog direction, but remains unapproved for automatic full-corpus execution. It creates identity/source links and payload provenance without refetching jobs or resetting first_seen_at. It does not erase the refill or compact storage. The existing --first-backfill command correctly refuses this partially mapped state; do not weaken or reuse it as a continuation runner.

The provenance discrepancy is resolved conservatively in code: all new inactive legacy sources become unknown, whether created by job mapping, account-only mapping or ordinary registration. A failure counter does not establish why a board became inactive: seeds can accumulate failures without automatic deactivation, and legacy activity reconciliation can change activity without clearing counters. Existing enabled, failure-disabled, deliberate and unknown states are preserved. The read-only audit found 2746 inactive companies at/above the failure threshold; these ambiguous boards require reviewed provenance before any recovery classification. They are not automatically reactivated by this change.

PR 20 implements an opt-in campaign child in the existing reviewer supervisor, using its existing database connection. The new additive migration creates only a private empty progress ledger. No campaign, mapping, control transition, readiness certificate or configuration activation is created by migration/deployment. The existing one-shot first-backfill command remains unchanged.

The worker pins an exact deployed revision, collect activation generation, absolute expiry, finite row/account/turn budgets and job/company high-water marks. Each transaction passes exact IDs to the mapper and commits mappings plus cursor/counters together. Account-only work has a separate keyset pass; accounts created while mapping jobs count against the same total account budget. A final bounded-corpus gap check catches concurrent inserts behind a cursor; completion is not a global readiness attestation. New IDs above high-water marks require another approved campaign. A real process-death test verifies that the last committed batch survives and resumes without duplication after lease expiry.

Bounds: 100 rows/transaction, at most 20 mapping transactions/turn, 240 seconds cooperative time, 1.25-second transaction window covering setup, mapping, finalization, cleanup and commit, with statement timeout clipped to the remaining window, 180 second renewed lease, 300 seconds hard process/supervisor limit, 240 CPU seconds, 384 MiB process address space, niceness 10 and a 60 second successful-turn cooldown. The supervisor checks for opted-in work every 60 seconds without overlapping its own identity children; a crash retains a 300 second retry timestamp and the consumed turn budget. Time-window exhaustion rolls back the uncommitted batch and remains resumable. Any other error, control/revision drift, expiry or capacity breach stops mapping; replaced/overlapping workers cannot stop an active successor. Pausing the campaign is observed before the next batch. Terminal campaigns make subsequent opted-in ticks read-only.

Before every batch, collect/identity-on controls and the exact generation must match; source, hydration, maintenance, feed, retirement and export must remain off, retirement dry run on, archive never activated. The database allocation plus held reservations plus a 32 MiB growth margin must be below 5800 MiB. A post-batch measurement at/above 5800 MiB rolls back that checkpoint and stops. This prevents further committed work; neither this margin nor rollback guarantees the database/volume cannot grow within a batch. WAL, temporary space and concurrent allocation need independent volume headroom and monitoring. No deletion or automatic capacity expansion is included.
